Aim and scope

Design a half adder and a full adder in Verilog-2001 from their Boolean equations, then verify every input combination of both circuits with a self-checking testbench.

A half adder adds two one-bit operands and produces a sum and a carry. It cannot accept a carry from a previous stage. A full adder adds three one-bit operands — a, b and cin — so it can be chained to build wider adders. That difference is the whole point of the experiment: the half adder is the simpler circuit, the full adder is the reusable stage.

Both modules are combinational and are written with continuous assignments. The testbench instantiates both, applies 4 half-adder vectors and 8 full-adder vectors, and compares each result against an independently computed integer value.

Learning objectives

  • Derive the sum and carry equations for a half adder from its truth table.
  • Extend the half adder to a full adder by introducing the propagate and generate terms.
  • Explain why the sum output is an XOR function while the carry output is a majority function.
  • Implement both circuits with dataflow assignments and named module ports.
  • Verify all 4 half-adder and all 8 full-adder input combinations.
  • Read a simulator transcript and distinguish a sum bit from the arithmetic value it represents.

Theory and working principle

Binary addition follows the same column rules as decimal addition. Adding two bits produces a one-bit sum and a one-bit carry:

  • 0 + 0 = 00 — sum 0, carry 0.
  • 0 + 1 = 01 and 1 + 0 = 01 — sum 1, carry 0.
  • 1 + 1 = 10 — sum 0, carry 1.

The sum column is 1 exactly when the inputs differ, which is XOR. The carry column is 1 only when both inputs are 1, which is AND. Those two observations give the half adder directly: sum = a ^ b and carry = a & b.

A full adder adds a third input, the carry from the less significant column. One way to reason about it is to add a and b first, producing an intermediate sum p = a ^ b and an intermediate carry g = a & b. Then add cin to p. The final sum is p ^ cin. A carry is produced either by the first addition (g) or by the second addition (p & cin), so carry = g | (p & cin).

The names p and g are conventional: p is the propagate term because a carry-in propagates through when p is 1, and g is the generate term because the stage generates a carry regardless of cin. This formulation is exactly what the design file implements, and it is what makes the full adder reusable in Experiment 04.

The full adder equations are not an arbitrary rewrite. Substituting p and g gives the standard form sum = a ^ b ^ cin and carry = (a & b) | (cin & (a ^ b)). Experiment 04 uses that observation to build a four-bit adder from four copies of this module.

Line-by-line explanation

Design

  • half_adder declares two inputs and two outputs. sum and carry are wires because both are driven by assign.
  • assign sum = a ^ b; implements the XOR column of the truth table.
  • assign carry = a & b; implements the AND column. At this point the half adder is complete.
  • full_adder adds cin and declares three internal wires. p and g hold the first-stage results.
  • assign sum = p ^ cin; adds the carry-in to the propagate term.
  • assign pc = p & cin; is the carry produced by the second stage.
  • assign carry = g | pc; merges both carry sources. A stage carries if it generated a carry or if it propagated an incoming one.

Testbench

  • The testbench declares separate reg drivers for each module, so the half adder and full adder can be exercised independently.
  • integer ha_value; and integer fa_value; hold the arithmetic result. An integer is used because the sum of three one-bit values can be 3, which needs two bits.
  • for (i = 0; i < 4; i = i + 1) covers the half adder; {ha_a, ha_b} = i[1:0] assigns the four input pairs in binary order.
  • for (i = 0; i < 8; i = i + 1) covers the full adder; {fa_a, fa_b, fa_cin} = i[2:0] walks 000 to 111.
  • ha_value = ha_a + ha_b; computes the expected value after the inputs are applied. Using an integer avoids the one-bit truncation that would make 1 + 1 print as 0.
  • if (ha_result !== ha_value) uses a four-state comparison, so an unknown output is counted as a failure.
  • #1 gives the continuous assignments one time unit to settle before the result is sampled.
  • $display prints one row per vector. The final line reports the mismatch count, and $finish ends the run.

Waveform and timing

There is no clock. Each vector is held for 1 ns, so the half-adder inputs change at 0, 1, 2 and 3 ns, and the full-adder inputs continue at 4 through 11 ns. The final $finish at 12,000 ps is 12 ns, not 12,000 ns.

Result

Both adders matched their truth tables.

The testbench checked 4 half-adder and 8 full-adder input combinations, 12 in total, and reported 0 mismatches. Every sum and carry output agrees with the equations and the diagram.

Because 4 and 8 vectors exhaust the input space of a two-input and a three-input circuit respectively, this is complete combinational verification. The result does not say anything about propagation delay or fan-out; those are timing questions, not logic questions.

Common mistakes and troubleshooting

Using OR for the carry

carry = a | b is wrong: it produces a carry for 0 + 1 and 1 + 0 as well as 1 + 1. The carry of a half adder is AND.

Forgetting the second carry source

In a full adder, carry = a & b misses the case where cin is added to a propagated 1. The OR with p & cin is required.

Truncating the arithmetic value

$display("%0d", a + b) with one-bit operands can print 0 for 1 + 1. Compute the expected value in an integer or wider vector.

Reusing one driver for both modules

If both modules share the same input regs, the half-adder and full-adder rows interfere. Keep separate drivers, as the testbench does.

Declaring outputs as reg

A reg cannot be the target of a continuous assignment. Use wire when every output is assigned with assign.

Misreading the finish time

Icarus prints the finish time in the precision unit. With `timescale 1ns / 1ps, 12000 means 12 ns.

Revision points

  • Half adder: sum = a ^ b, carry = a & b.
  • Full adder: sum = a ^ b ^ cin, carry = (a & b) | (cin & (a ^ b)).
  • The propagate/generate form exposes the structure that makes wide adders possible.
  • Four vectors exhaust a two-input circuit; eight vectors exhaust a three-input circuit.
  • An integer expected value prevents width truncation in the transcript.
  • !== catches unknown outputs that != might miss.
